ORDERING, PROVED NOT GUESSED: the per-head norm is applied BEFORE RoPE, for Q
and for K. Lines 88 and 97 are the norms; lines 91 and 100 are the ropes; the
norm line is strictly earlier in both pairs. An implementation that normed after
RoPE would consume the WRONG tensor: RoPE rotates pairs of components, and RMS
norm over a rotated vector is a different operation with a different result. The
reference even names the intermediate "Qcur_normed" via its debug callback, so
the order is visible in the source rather than only in its effect.

THE NORM IS RMS, not LayerNorm. qwen3.cpp passes LLM_NORM_RMS, and
llama-graph.cpp:1600 maps that to ggml_rms_norm(ctx0, cur,
hparams.f_norm_rms_eps). No mean subtraction and no bias: the bias slots
attn_q_norm_b and attn_k_norm_b exist in llama-model.h but are not created by
qwen3.cpp, and this GGUF carries no ".bias" tensors for them.

HEAD DIMENSION IS 128, and it is stated three independent ways: key_length says
128, the tensor shape is { n_embd_head_k }, and the captured tensors are
ne = [128, 1] F32. Q HEADS 32, KV HEADS 8.

WHY THIS MATTERS NUMERICALLY, and it is not just an extra call. The norm
reduces 128 head elements, not n_embd 4096, so it is 32x narrower than every
norm in the BitNet graph. It is applied PER HEAD, and because head_count is 32
while head_count_kv is 8, the 8 KV heads each need their own K norm; a K norm
shared across heads or applied once to the whole 1024-wide K would be wrong.
And a shared attn_k_norm.weight of ne [128,1] is indexed by head.
